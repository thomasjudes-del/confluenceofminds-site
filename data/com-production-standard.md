# Confluence of Minds - Social Production Standard

This file is the binding production standard for all future Confluence of Minds social creatives.

## 1. Delivery reliability is part of quality
- The actual video preview is the primary approval asset.
- Before presenting any candidate, verify the final MP4 exists, is non-zero, opens with ffprobe, contains an H.264/H.265 video stream and the intended audio stream, and has the expected duration/dimensions.
- Re-copy/re-export the final asset to a simple ASCII filename before delivery.
- Deliver the preview as a directly openable ChatGPT attachment/download link. Do not rely on a filename, contact sheet, Drive URL, or descriptive text.
- If Thomas reports a broken link, immediately regenerate/re-upload a fresh preview file before explaining anything else.
- Never claim a preview is ready unless the exact file being linked was verified after the final render.

## 2. Music and sound must be editorial choices
- Never use white noise, placeholder ambience, test tones, crude drones, accidental silence, or synthetic filler as a soundtrack.
- Never use cheap MIDI-sounding melodies or mock-instrument imitations in an approval candidate.
- Prefer, in order:
  1. owned project music/audio;
  2. a high-quality properly licensed or royalty-free track with verified usage rights;
  3. a genuinely musical generated score that sounds like a finished soundtrack, not a sketch.
- Soundtrack selection must fit the specific world and tone. Avoid generic corporate music, tourist-cliche "ethnic" music, or obvious stock cliches.
- Check that music is clearly audible on phone playback and that the final mix has sensible loudness and no clipping.
- If semantic listening is unavailable, state that limitation. Do not substitute poor audio because listening is unavailable.

## 3. New content means new editorial value
- Google Drive `Confluence RS` is the canonical source library, not a playlist to recycle forever.
- Check recent publication history before ideation.
- Do not repost the same teaser/master or a near-identical derivative once it has already been published recently.
- Reusing a world is allowed only with a materially new angle, scene, character, symbol, question, theme, visual motif, worldbuilding detail, or format.
- Actively search for editorial blind spots in each project.
- Create new images, shots, motion pieces, compositions or audio when existing material is insufficient.
- Existing assets should serve as canon, art direction and ingredients for new work.

## 4. Trailer editing: condense, never truncate
- Inspect the entire source master before editing.
- A social cut must be a true remontage using the strongest material from across the whole source.
- Never just take the first N seconds or chop the tail.
- Preserve the dramatic progression, escalation, strongest images, climax and audio identity.
- Use enough distinct shots to feel deliberately edited.
- Do not let setup footage dominate while later high-value shots disappear.

## 5. Pacing and duration
- There is no fixed 8-second rule.
- Use whatever duration makes the creative intelligible, compelling and aesthetically complete.
- 15, 20, 30, 40 seconds or more are acceptable when justified.
- Text must remain on screen long enough to read comfortably once on a phone.
- If reading feels rushed, extend the shot or reduce the text.
- The piece should have a beginning, development, rise and conclusion/hero ending when appropriate.

## 6. Typography and on-screen text
- Typography is part of the art direction, not a utility overlay.
- Use a typeface, size, spacing and placement that fit the world.
- Avoid generic subtitles, ugly boxes, crude all-caps treatments and text pasted at the top of the frame without a visual reason.
- Text must never be cropped, clipped, too close to UI zones or obstruct important imagery.
- Check first, middle and final frames in a phone-sized 9:16 composition.
- Keep copy short and factual. Never invent plot, mechanics or meaning.
- Do not overlay explanatory text on a final poster/title card when the poster already contains the intended title.

## 7. Framing and visual preservation
- Never stretch source footage.
- Avoid destructive crops that remove meaningful content.
- For 9:16 adaptation, use intelligent crop only when safe.
- Otherwise use coherent background fill, padding, or a native redesign that preserves the source image and aesthetic intent.
- Final shots should be chosen for visual authority, not convenience.
- When an official poster/key art is strong, use it as the ending when it strengthens the piece.

## 8. Visual creation quality
- New AI-generated visuals must be project-specific and coherent with established art direction.
- Reject generic gradients, obvious filler, repetitive layouts, cheap template motion and visual cliches.
- Create additional visuals when needed to enrich pacing and prevent a slideshow feel.
- Favor a small number of strong, authored shots over many weak ones.

## 9. QC before approval
A candidate is approval-ready only if:
- source fidelity is verified;
- it is not a recent duplicate;
- the rendered final asset itself has been inspected;
- the video file is directly openable;
- safe zones pass;
- typography is intentional;
- pacing is readable;
- music is a real editorial choice and audible;
- no accidental silence/placeholder audio remains;
- rights/factual basis are checked;
- mobile 9:16 preview is acceptable.
If any of these fail, mark REWORK and fix before asking Thomas to approve.

## 10. Approval workflow
- One master creative per day by default.
- Present the actual playable preview first.
- Keep the approval pack compact.
- Thomas responds APPROVE / REWORK / NO-GO.
- Never publish, schedule or queue before explicit approval in chat.
- After approval, publish only to the approved channels and log the publication and creative angle in GitHub.

## Core principle
A Confluence social asset must feel authored, world-specific and premium. It is better to ship one strong piece than a fast recycled montage with weak typography, poor framing, generic music or broken delivery.
